How to read it

  • The gap narrows from $1.96 to $0.11 by 2040, then stalls.
  • Liquefier CAPEX and large LH₂ tanks carry the closure. Liquefaction energy carries very little.
  • Parity needs every target on schedule and NH₃ standing still.

Caveats to resolve before external use (mock-up, plus our review)

  • KBR figures include blue-hydrogen production and are volume scenarios, not calendar years, so they are not like-for-like with the 2026–2045 path above.
  • The Roland Berger "near parity by 2035" claim could not be verified and is excluded (mock-up).
  • KHI tank cost, carrier price and guaranteed liquefier energy are unpublished (mock-up); our database confirms carrier price (Q31) as undisclosed and tank/terminal CAPEX as available only as an IAE aggregate.
  • IEA ETP 2023 figures were taken second-hand via Hydrogen Insight, and arXiv 2507.02412 is a preprint giving only percentage edges.

The two bases differ in vintage, scale and what "other" and "losses" contain, so read the ratio as order of magnitude only.

Why this matters. Our chain-summary dashboard (energy cost only, no CAPEX) puts LH₂ within about ±2% of the cheapest NH₃ route at maturity (LH₂ at 6 kWh/kg: USD 10.60/kg vs NH₃ NG-fired, VLSFO-only ship: USD 10.82/kg; both include electrolysis at placeholder prices). The mock-up reaches a gap of $0.1–2.0 per kg only once CAPEX is included. So the carrier decision is a CAPEX question, and CAPEX is the part KHI has not disclosed. A pre-FEED or feasibility scope with KHI is the single action that would most improve this dashboard's accuracy.

  1. Gather. Zane's weekly LH₂ digest (Mondays 07:00 MYT) and OEM or developer engagement feed candidate updates into the research staging area.
  2. Review. Custodian checks each candidate: source, origin, and whether it contradicts an internal figure. Proposes an accuracy mark.
