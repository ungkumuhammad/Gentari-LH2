Method, assumptions and gaps

What the upstream numbers rest on

  • Production follows the ship. The liquefier makes one LH₂ cargo: 40,000 m³ × 70.8 kg/m³ at the Shipping sheet's fill. The ammonia plant makes one NH₃ cargo: 40,000 m³ × 682 kg/m³. Its H₂ feed is cargo ÷ stoichiometric ratio, so the NH₃ route consumes more H₂ per cargo.
  • Haber-Bosch at 1 kWh/kg H₂ is your figure, equal to 0.18 kWh/kg NH₃. The repo's placeholder is 0.5–0.7 kWh/kg NH₃, about 2.8–3.9 kWh/kg H₂, and includes the air separation unit (ASU) for N₂, syngas compression and refrigeration [ESTIMATE]. Check whether your 1 kWh/kg includes the ASU. If not, the NH₃ bar is understated.
  • No synthesis heat credit. Ammonia synthesis is exothermic and can raise steam. That credit is excluded, as agreed for the chain study.
  • Tank boil-off. Taken on the full 60,000 m³ tank for the storage days per cargo, linear in days. BOR applies to the inventory held, not to the cargo size. LH₂ boil-off is re-liquefied at 3.94 kWh/kg, a condensation-only duty derived in the repo [ASSUMPTION]. Set it to 9 to charge full liquefaction. NH₃ reliq at 0.25 kWh/kg is a repo placeholder [ESTIMATE].
  • Storage days per cargo depends on plant output and ship schedule, neither of which is fixed yet. Five days is the repo's placeholder [ASSUMPTION].
  • Not included: electrolysis (same GH₂ feed on both sides, but the NH₃ route needs more of it per cargo), loading pumps and arms, flare or vent losses.

Assessment · H₂ price

At what H₂ price does burning boil-off still beat the ammonia ship?

The LH₂ carrier has no re-liquefaction, so every kilogram that boils off leaves the cargo. It is either burned in the engine or, above the crossover, sent to the GCU or vented. Its cost is the hydrogen's sale value. The ammonia carrier re-liquefies its boil-off and buys ammonia fuel plus a 5 % VLSFO pilot.

Method, assumptions and gaps

What the numbers rest on

  • Energy parity. One MJ of H₂ is assumed to displace one MJ of VLSFO in the engine (LHV basis, equal engine efficiency on either fuel). [ASSUMPTION] A dual-fuel engine's efficiency on gas and on oil is not held in the repo.
  • Pilot fuel. Your table lets VLSFO fall to zero. Dual-fuel engines normally keep a small liquid pilot. Set a pilot % in the inputs to see the crossover move right. No sourced pilot figure for the KHI engine is held. [ESTIMATE – needs source]
  • VLSFO LHV of 41.0 MJ/kg is back-calculated from your table (it reproduces your VLSFO column to the second decimal). The repo's existing shipping study uses 40.2 MJ/kg [ESTIMATE]. At 40.2 the crossover moves to about 0.461 %/day.
  • Full cargo, laden leg. Boil-off is taken on 100 % of 40,000 m³. With the repo's 98 % fill the boil-off is 2 % lower and the crossover about 0.480 %/day. The ballast leg (heel only) is not modelled.
  • NH₃ carrier. The 0.15 %/day boil-off is re-liquefied back to cargo, so it causes no cargo loss. Reliquefaction power is assumed to sit inside the 30 t/d demand. [ASSUMPTION]
  • Prices. VLSFO and NH₃ prices default to USD 600/t as placeholders [ASSUMPTION]. They are not market quotes. Replace them with your corridor prices. The H₂ breakeven follows directly from them (Chart 4).
  • Not included: carbon price or FuelEU penalties, N₂O slip from ammonia combustion, and the CO₂e of vented surplus H₂. All three would shift the breakeven. None has a sourced value in this repo.

Method, assumptions and gaps

What the downstream numbers rest on

  • The ORV heat is free. The 3.8 MJ/kg regas duty comes from ambient seawater. The only purchased energy is the pumps that move LH₂ and seawater kawasaki-2026-questionnaire. The pump settings (30 barg, 70 %, ΔT 5 K, 20 m, 75 %) are the repo's first-principles assumptions. They reproduce its 0.031 kWh/kg ORV figure [ASSUMPTION].
  • Other regas auxiliaries not included: seawater electro-chlorination, lighting and control power, and the cold energy an ORV throws away. No sourced figures are held for them [ESTIMATE – needs source]. They are small next to the pumps.
  • Cracker firing modes. In NG-fired mode, 6.5 − 5.6 = 0.9 kg NH₃/kg H₂ does not become product. It burns in the furnace as PSA tail gas. Natural gas supplies the remaining 7.2 − 6.5 = 0.7 kg NH₃-eq. Clean-fuel mode fires 7.2 − 5.6 = 1.6 kg NH₃/kg H₂ with no gas. Total fired energy is the same in both modes by construction: 1.6 × 18.6 = 29.8 MJ/kg H₂.
  • Thermodynamic sanity check. The minimum cracking heat is 15.2 MJ/kg H₂ (45.9 kJ/mol NH₃, repo [needs source]). Firing 29.8 MJ implies about 51 % of the fired heat goes into the reaction. The rest vaporises and preheats the feed or leaves up the stack. That split is plausible, so the fuel rates pass the check.
  • Mixed energy quality. The cracker bars add fuel on an LHV basis to electricity. Regas is electricity only. A kWh of electricity is worth more than a kWh of fuel heat, so these bars compare quantity of energy, not primary energy.
  • Same send-out pressure assumed. Cracker H₂ is taken to leave the PSA at the same pressure as the LH₂ send-out. No extra H₂ compression is added to either side [ASSUMPTION].
  • Ship basis. Each bar is one full 40,000 m³ cargo before voyage losses. The LH₂ ship lands 2,832 t of H₂. The NH₃ ship lands 27,280 t NH₃, which yields cargo ÷ feed rate of H₂. That is less H₂ in clean-fuel mode, because more cargo is burned.
  • Stoichiometry. Your 5.6 rounds the exact 2 × 17.031 ÷ (3 × 2.016) = 5.632. With 5.632 the NH₃ fuel term in NG-fired mode drops about 3.6 % (0.868 vs 0.9 kg/kg), and total fired energy drops 2 % in both modes (1.568 vs 1.6 kg NH₃-eq/kg H₂).

Method, assumptions and gaps

What the chain numbers rest on

  • Per kg H₂ delivered divides total chain energy by the gaseous H₂ that leaves the import terminal. For LH₂ that is cargo less voyage boil-off. For NH₃ it is cargo ÷ cracker feed rate (6.5 or 7.2 kg NH₃/kg H₂).
  • Voyage. Sea days × the Shipping sheet's daily figures, linear in days as on that sheet. Twenty laden days is a placeholder [ASSUMPTION]. No corridor is fixed yet. The ballast leg is off by default. When on, each ship burns its full daily demand as VLSFO for the same number of days, and heel boil-off is ignored [ASSUMPTION].
  • Hydrogen burned on the LH₂ ship counts as energy used. It is cargo H₂ at 120 MJ/kg, whether the engine burns it or, above the crossover rate, the GCU does.
  • NH₃ vessel fuel is selectable. Dual fuel burns the Shipping sheet's NH₃ share (95 %) of the 30 t/d VLSFO-equivalent demand as ammonia, with the rest as VLSFO pilot. VLSFO only burns the whole demand as VLSFO. Ship energy per day is the same in both. By default the ammonia fuel is bunkered separately and the cargo arrives whole (boil-off re-liquefied) [ASSUMPTION]. Taking it from cargo moves that energy into "cargo burned", shrinks the cargo that reaches the cracker, and lowers H₂ delivered per cargo.
  • Downstream on the delivered cargo. Regas is charged on the LH₂ that arrives, so it is slightly lower here than on the Downstream sheet, which uses the full cargo.
  • Energy quality is mixed. Electricity, fuel heat and burned hydrogen are added on an LHV basis. This is quantity of energy, not primary energy or exergy. Electricity-heavy segments (liquefaction) would weigh more on a primary-energy basis.
  • Electrolysis is off by default because both chains start from the same GH₂. Switch it on to charge the H₂ each chain consumes, which penalises the route that loses more hydrogen per kg delivered. 60 kWh/kg is the repo's user-set placeholder [ASSUMPTION].
  • All upstream, shipping and downstream caveats on the other sheets carry over, notably the Haber-Bosch 1 kWh/kg H₂ question (ASU included?) and the placeholder reliquefaction and storage-day values.

Method, assumptions and gaps

What the case numbers rest on

  • Scaling. Annual energy = Full Chain energy per kg H₂ delivered × contracted volume. Cargoes per year = contract ÷ H₂ delivered per cargo, kept fractional for energy. A part cargo is treated as part of a year-round flow.
  • Fleet. Round trip = 2 × sea days + 2 × port days. The ballast leg is assumed to take as long as the laden leg, whether or not its fuel is charged on the Full Chain sheet. Voyages per ship per year = 365 × availability ÷ round trip. Ships = cargoes per year ÷ voyages per ship, rounded up. The 40,000 m³ hull is used for both chains. Port days are the KHI 1–1.5-day midpoint kawasaki-2026-questionnaire. Availability 90 % is a repo assumption [ASSUMPTION].
  • NH₃ vessel fuel. Dual fuel burns the Shipping sheet's NH₃ share (95 %) of the 30 t/d VLSFO-equivalent demand as ammonia, and the rest as VLSFO pilot. VLSFO only burns the full demand as VLSFO. When ammonia fuel is taken from cargo, the ship arrives with less NH₃. Cracking energy then scales down with the smaller cargo, H₂ delivered per cargo falls, and more cargoes are needed. The plant still makes a full cargo. Heel and boil-off are not modelled.
  • Export tank boil-off is charged per cargo (storage days per cargo on the Upstream sheet) and scales with cargoes per year. A tank farm holding inventory all year would boil off more. Tank count is not sized here.
  • Plant capacity is annual output ÷ 365 at 100 % utilisation. A real plant needs margin for availability and turndown [ASSUMPTION].
  • Every caveat on the Full Chain, Upstream, Shipping and Downstream sheets carries over. That includes the Haber-Bosch 1 kWh/kg H₂ question, 20 placeholder sea days, and mixed energy quality on an LHV basis.

Method, assumptions and gaps

What the cost numbers rest on

  • Energy cost only. Each energy line from the Full Chain is priced at its own carrier price. Charter hire, CapEx, fixed OpEx, port fees, carbon cost and the price of the H₂ feed itself are excluded.
  • Losses only. Hydrogen and ammonia are charged only where they are burned: LH₂ boil-off at the H₂ price; ammonia burned on the ship and in the cracker at the ammonia price. H₂ feed into the plant is not charged, so neither route pays for the extra H₂ it needs per kg delivered (switch on electrolysis on the Full Chain sheet to charge that as electricity).
  • Natural gas is the cracker's NH₃-equivalent LHV gas demand, converted at 1,055.056 MJ/MMBtu and priced at 13 USD/MMBtu with no HHV adjustment, as you chose. Gas priced on an HHV basis would cost about 10 % more [ESTIMATE – needs source].
  • Electricity. Upstream covers liquefaction, Haber-Bosch, export-tank reliquefaction and electrolysis if switched on. Import-terminal electricity covers regas pumps, import-tank reliquefaction and the cracker's 0.29 kWh/kg. The import price is a placeholder equal to upstream until you set it [ASSUMPTION].
  • Separate from the Shipping sheet. The prices here are separate from the Shipping sheet's VLSFO and NH₃ inputs, which only drive its ship-day breakeven.
  • Volumes, fuel mode, sea days and every energy assumption come from the Case Study and Full Chain sheets and carry their caveats.

Method, assumptions and gaps

What the cost numbers rest on

  • Denominator. Annual energy cost ÷ H₂ produced by the electrolyser. That is the H₂ into the liquefier or the Haber-Bosch loop: 2,832 t per LH₂ cargo, and NH₃ cargo ÷ stoichiometric ratio per NH₃ cargo. The Economics sheet divides the same annual bill by H₂ delivered instead.
  • Same money, different yardstick. USD M per year is identical on both sheets. Only the per-kg figure changes. The route that loses more H₂ between electrolyser and delivery looks relatively cheaper here, because its bill is spread over more kilograms produced.
  • Volume. The electrolyser output is whatever the Case Study's delivered volume requires: contract × H₂ in ÷ H₂ delivered for each chain.
  • All costing choices from the Economics sheet carry over: energy only, losses only, H₂ feed not charged, natural gas on LHV, and the import-terminal electricity placeholder.
