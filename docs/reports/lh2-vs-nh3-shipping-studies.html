LH2 vs Ammonia at Sea

Both ships have to move themselves as well as their cargo. The ammonia carrier burns 25 t/day of VLSFO and re-liquefies its boil-off. The hydrogen carrier burns its own cargo instead — so the real question is whether that boil-off covers the fuel bill, falls short of it, or overshoots it, and what the hydrogen given up is worth.

Does the boil-off cover the fuel bill?

Sweeping: LH₂ boil-off rate

Kawasaki says the carrier's fuel-gas consumption equals its boil-off rate. That only closes if the boil-off happens to match what the engine wants. Below the demand line the ship still buys bunker fuel; above it, boil-off arrives faster than the engine can burn it and the excess leaves the ship having done no work at all.

What is not sourced here

  1. LH₂ voyage boil-off rate. Kawasaki disclosed none — only that fuel-gas consumption equals the boil-off rate. 0.2 %/day is a placeholder; the nearest public figure for a comparable vessel (RSER, 2026) is ~3.44 %/day. Everything on this page turns on it.
  2. The 25 t/day propulsion duty is your figure for the ammonia carrier, and it is charged to the LH₂ carrier as well so the two are on one basis. The LH₂ vessel is a larger hull at ~23 % higher speed, so its true demand is probably higher — which would absorb more boil-off usefully and raise its top-up fuel bill.
  3. Hydrogen and bunker prices are yours to set. Neither is logged in the repository, and no default is asserted as correct — the study reports the breakeven price instead.
  4. Every ammonia vessel figure — speed, capacity, port time, boil-off, re-liquefaction energy, genset efficiency — is user-stated or estimated, pending the internal Gentari NH₃ dataset (decision D4).
  5. Surplus disposal. The page defaults to venting, per your framing. Kawasaki's own reply Q26 describes a gas combustion unit burning excess boil-off above MARVS — in which case the hydrogen is oxidised to water and the warming figure does not apply. The hydrogen is lost from the cargo either way; only the environmental line changes. Toggle it in the detail panel.
  6. Vented-hydrogen warming potential (11.6 kg CO₂e/kg, 100-year) is an estimate with a wide published uncertainty band and no primary citation logged.
  7. Ballast leg excluded for both vessels. Propulsion fuel is charged over the laden leg only, where the boil-off is generated. Including it would raise both carriers' fuel bills and dilute the LH₂ carrier's displacement credit.
  8. VLSFO LHV (40.2 MJ/kg) is the marine convention for residual fuel, not a citation from the repository's reference registry.
