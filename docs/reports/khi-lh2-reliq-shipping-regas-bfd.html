    26. BOG management energy is derived, not KHI-disclosed. BOG is already at ~20.3 K and near-equilibrium para fraction when it boils off, so its ideal (theoretical minimum) energy is only the condensation step (≈1.71 kWh/kg), not the full fresh-feed cool-down + ortho-para conversion (≈3.9 kWh/kg, para-H2 basis) — see docs/methodology/02-liquefaction.md "BOG re-liquefaction is a different (smaller) calculation." This calculator scales your own reliquefaction SEC input by the ratio of those two ideal-work figures (≈0.438) to estimate a real-world BOG-management specific energy, applied to export-terminal, import-terminal, and shipping boil-off for consistent energy accounting — even though KHI's ships actually burn voyage BOG as propulsion fuel rather than electrically re-liquefying it (reply Q23/Q32).     27. Reliquefaction plant stream days/year is a separate, user-editable input (default 365).     28. Reliquefaction plant card (②) totals only fresh-feed liquefaction energy + export/loading-terminal BOG re-liq energy — not shipping or import-terminal BOG. KHI's replies (Q3/Q14) describe BOG re-liquefaction as happening via the liquefier's own ejector or a cryogenic-rated compressor feeding back into the liquefaction process, specifically for BOG "generated in downstream sections of the liquefaction terminal" (i.e. the export/loading terminal) — not for shipping BOG (burned as propulsion fuel, note 12/18) or import-terminal BOG (a different site, KHI's reliquefaction reply doesn't address it — note 22). Average power draw on this card is fresh-feed + export-terminal-BOG energy combined, divided by 8,760 h/y. Energy figures on this card are shown in kWh/y (MJ/y) per CLAUDE.md §5's kWh/MJ convention, rather than the GWh/TJ used elsewhere in this diagram for larger chain-wide totals. The separate "Well-to-Gate energy efficiency" card below still sums BOG energy across all three locations (export + shipping + import) for whole-chain energy-input accounting — that is a different accounting purpose from this plant-level card. ASSUMPTION: derived scope
    29. Terminal tank sizing and BOG are vessel-speed/fleet-driven and volume-driven, not user-typed hold-days. Export/loading and import/receiving terminal cards each recommend a "survival size" tank capacity: the larger of (a) one full vessel cargo parcel (since loading/discharge happens in a single batch) or (b) daily production/offtake x survival days, where survival_days = loading_interval_days + vessel_arrival_buffer_days. loading_interval_days = round_trip_days / fleet_size is the fleet's average time between calls (round-trip depends on the vessel speed and distance inputs); vessel_arrival_buffer_days (default 2 d, in-vessel-buffer) is a small explicit contingency for a late vessel. This is deliberately leaner than sizing for a full missed round-trip cycle — the fleet is already sized to the throughput (see fleetSize()), so a worst-case single-vessel-failure basis would recommend needlessly large (costly) tanks; a fleet-normalized interval plus a modest lateness buffer better reflects how a multi-vessel operation is actually run, at the cost of speed/distance mattering less once the fleet auto-scales to compensate (per fleetSize(), the interval converges toward roughly cargo capacity x fleet-availability regardless of speed) — the buffer-days term is what keeps some schedule-risk margin without inflating the whole tank. No blanket safety margin is layered on top. Recommended sizes are pre-filled but fully editable (in-export-capacity, in-import-capacity) — leave blank to use the recommendation, or type your own (e.g. a smaller value if you want to test a leaner/riskier design, or a larger one for extra schedule slack). Whichever capacity is in effect then drives BOG directly: average residence time = tank inventory (capacity x LH2 density) / daily mass flow (the standard CSTR/space-time relationship, tau = V/Q), fed into the same exponential boil-off-at-rest formula used elsewhere (note 7/16) — a larger tank relative to throughput means LH2 dwells longer and accumulates more boil-off. None of this sizing methodology is KHI-disclosed — KHI only gives the actual Base/Large-scale tank counts and unit sizes (notes 7, 16), not a design/sizing method; treat every survival-size, buffer-day, and residence-time figure as a project-specific engineering assumption to be checked against a real terminal design and cost model. ASSUMPTION: derived

    31. Regasification plant card (⑥) now derives the ORV seawater pump's electrical energy from first principles — no KHI or public source gives an electrical consumption figure for KHI's disclosed ORV/seawater vaporizer (reply Q17 covers only the heat-source choice; reply Q18 gives the 3.8 MJ/kg thermal duty, which is free ambient heat, not an electrical cost). The derivation: (1) seawater mass flow = heat duty / (seawater specific heat x your Regas seawater ΔT input, default 5°C — a typical LNG/LH2 ORV design temperature drop, not a KHI figure); (2) seawater volume = mass / seawater density; (3) pump hydraulic energy = seawater density x gravity x your Regas seawater pump head input (default 20 m) x volume; (4) electrical input = hydraulic energy / your Regas seawater pump efficiency input (default 75%). An optional Regas supplemental heater SEC input (default 0, i.e. seawater-only, matching KHI's disclosed design) adds a second electrical term if you want to model backup/trim electric heating. The resulting total is folded into the "Well-to-Gate energy efficiency" card below as a fourth input-energy term alongside electrolyzer, reliquefaction, and BOG management — which is also why that card (and the KPI tile) is renamed from "Well-to-LH2" to "Well-to-Gate", since the diagram's declared boundary already runs electrolyzer battery limit → regas GH₂ send-out, and this energy term now actually covers that last leg. Every ΔT/head/efficiency default here is a placeholder for your own design basis, not a sourced or KHI-derived value — treat the whole regas electrical figure as approximate until validated against a real ORV pump curve/vendor datasheet. ASSUMPTION: derived
    32. Regas send-out spec (30°C / 30 barg default) and its two energy consequences, re-checked. The regas card now takes explicit Regas send-out temperature and Regas send-out pressure inputs (defaults 30°C / 30 barg, mirroring the same 30°C/30 barg used for the electrolyzer feed condition — note 21 — as a symmetric Gentari project spec, not a KHI figure). Two consequences are modeled: (1) Pressure — cryogenic pump work, shown as "Cryo pump-to-pressure": LH2 is pumped from ~1 atm storage pressure up to send-out pressure before vaporization (specific work = ΔP / LH2 density / pump efficiency) — the standard, efficient design choice, since pumping liquid (nearly incompressible) costs far less than compressing the equivalent gas; no separate gas compressor is modeled, on the assumption pressurization always happens on the liquid side. This is folded into "Total regas electrical energy" alongside the seawater pump (note 31). (2) Temperature — heat duty cross-check, shown as "Heat duty cross-check": an independent order-of-magnitude estimate = latent heat at NBP (≈446 kJ/kg) + sensible heat from NBP (20.369 K) to your target temperature, using a constant gas specific heat of ≈14.3 kJ/kg·K (the standard room-temperature H2 value). This does not replace KHI's cited 3.8 MJ/kg (reply Q18), which still drives the seawater-flow calc — it is shown only as a sanity check, and it is a known over-estimate: real H2 gas Cp is lower than 14.3 kJ/kg·K at cryogenic temperatures (quantum rotational modes freeze out approaching 0 K), so the true sensible-heat integral is smaller than a constant-Cp calculation gives. A NIST WebBook fetch for exact real-gas enthalpies at both end states was attempted for this session and blocked (HTTP 403); the 446 kJ/kg and 14.3 kJ/kg·K figures come from published secondary sources (nuclear-power.com, engineeringtoolbox.com, Wikipedia heat-capacity data), not NIST directly. Do not treat the cross-check number as authoritative — it exists to flag whether KHI's figure is in the right ballpark for a ~30°C target (it is, by inspection: KHI's 3.8 MJ/kg sits below this over-estimate, consistent with real Cp being lower than the constant 14.3 used here) — get real REFPROP/NIST enthalpy data before using it for anything beyond a screening check.
